Analysis plan: Cryptococcosis in kidney transplant recipients, rebuilt with usRds

Status: draft. Not locked.

Scope and reporting framework

This document is the authoritative specification of the study’s scientific objectives, population, definitions, estimands and statistical methods. ARCHITECTURE.md documents the implementation, AGENTS.md the working conventions, and GitHub Issues the open decisions and implementation status. Neither file supersedes this plan.

The analysis replicates a published study1 as the paper describes it, using the usRds package wherever it can do a step, to assess the package’s coverage. The paper and its Supplementary Table 1 are the specification. The original analysis was run in Stata, and that code is no longer available; a later R Markdown reconstruction (2025-03-25) is used only as secondary evidence of how a step may have been done. Where the paper is silent, the plan records an open decision rather than adopting the reconstruction’s choice unexamined. The published estimates are a reference point, not a target. How each step maps to usRds is in references/usrds-coverage.md.

Reporting will follow STROBE2 and RECORD3. The reporting map in §12 links each checklist item to a section of this plan.

1. Objective

  • Primary: estimate all-cause mortality within 6 months of the index cryptococcosis hospitalization among adult kidney transplant recipients hospitalized under Medicare, 2006–2016.
  • Secondary:
    • describe time from kidney transplant to the index hospitalization;
    • estimate the association of clinical, transplant and immunosuppression characteristics with 6-month mortality.
  • Methodological (this project): assess, step by step, whether usRds does what the paper’s methods require (covered, partial or gap).

All analyses are exploratory, as in the paper. No hypotheses are tested confirmatorily.

2. Design

Retrospective cohort of kidney transplant recipients, entering at their first post-transplant cryptococcosis hospitalization (the index hospitalization). The question is predictive and descriptive (which factors are associated with death), not causal. Target-trial emulation is not used.

3. Data source

  • Database: United States Renal Data System standard analysis files, with linked Medicare claims and UNOS transplant records. The release is recorded with each result. The paper used a release covering 2006–2016 claims.
  • Files:
    • core: patients, tx, payer history;
    • hospitalization: hosp_to2009, hosp_2010on;
    • institutional and physician/supplier claims;
    • UNOS: txunos_trr_ki, txunos_trr_kp, txfuunos_ki, txfuunos_kp, txirunos.
  • Observation window: index hospitalizations admitted 2006-01-01 to 2016-12-31. Deaths are ascertained through the release’s death-date currency.
  • Linkage: by USRDS_ID, as provided by USRDS. Transplant status comes from USRDS core and UNOS files, not diagnosis codes.
  • Secondary-data declaration: the investigators have analysed these data before (the published study). Results from this reconstruction will be compared with the paper only to check package behaviour.

The index hospitalization and its stay-level variables (diagnoses, procedures, ICU and CCU days, discharge status) come from the USRDS hospitalization files, hosp_to2009 and hosp_2010on (decided in #1). These files are a subset of the institutional claims, restricted to inpatient stays without payment variables (Researcher’s Guide 2024, Appendix B), so institutional claims are not used as a second source for the index stay.

4. Study population

Source population: recipients of a kidney or kidney–pancreas transplant in the USRDS tx file. Exclusions are applied in this order, and each becomes one flowchart step (Figure 1):

  1. Hospitalizations with a cryptococcosis diagnosis code (§5) in any diagnosis position, admitted 2006-01-01 to 2016-12-31.
  2. Restrict to people in the transplant file.
  3. Exclude hospitalizations admitted before the patient’s first kidney transplant. A hospitalization admitted before the transplant is a cryptococcosis hospitalization claim preceding it, which the paper excludes; this includes stays that begin before the transplant and end after it (decided under #4; the reconstruction kept such stays with time since transplant set to 0).
  4. Exclude people with any cryptococcosis claim before their first kidney transplant. Hospital records and physician/supplier claims both count, as the paper says (“physician or hospitalization”), and one claim is enough (decided under #4). A single claim is the usual choice for excluding an earlier condition, where missing a true earlier case matters more than excluding a few people over a rule-out code4. A claim dated on the transplant day is not before it. The paper also excludes claims “before the study period”; the data begin on 2006-01-01, so there are none to find.
  5. Keep each person’s first remaining cryptococcosis hospitalization (the index hospitalization).
  6. Restrict to age 18–90 years on the index admission date.

No separate Medicare-primary restriction is applied. The paper describes the cohort as recipients “with Medicare as their primary insurance coverage” but gives no window or method, and the index hospitalization is itself a Medicare-paid record. The plan therefore treats Medicare-primary status as a consequence of the data source rather than an applied exclusion (decided in #3). An explicit restriction is described as a variation in §9.

The paper does not state the order of step 6 relative to the others. The order above puts claim-based exclusions first, so each later step’s denominator is a set of people with an index hospitalization.

5. Exposure, index event and covariates

Index event and time zero: admission date (CLM_FROM) of the index hospitalization. Overlapping or contiguous hospitalization records are one stay; the stay ends at the last record’s CLM_THRU.

Code lists: from the 2025-03-25 reconstruction of the original analysis, by the maintainer’s decision (2026-11-09, #8), rather than from Supplementary Table 11. The two are identical except for diabetes and the cyclosporine and tacrolimus HCPCS codes, which only the supplement lists and which are not used, because immunosuppression comes from the UNOS records (#2). After checking every code against the official code sets, three corrections were made (maintainer’s decision, 2026-11-09): diabetes stems match as open prefixes, as in the supplement, because the reconstruction’s enumeration (stem plus at most two digits) misses most ICD-10-CM diabetes codes; ICD-9-CM 456.20 is added to cirrhosis, missing from both sources; and a ventriculoperitoneal shunt list is rebuilt (below). Codes are written without decimals and match exactly, except the diabetes prefixes. ICD-9-CM applies to inpatient stays discharged before 2015-10-01 and ICD-10-CM/PCS to those discharged on or after it, following CMS’s discharge-date rule; for the hospitalization files the discharge date is CLM_THRU. Lists are kept in code with their provenance (references/usrds-coverage.md).

Concept Codes

Cryptococcal meningitis (CM) 321.0, B45.1
Transplant complication 996.81, T86.10–T86.12
Acute kidney injury 584.5–584.9, N17.0–N17.2, N17.8, N17.9

Covariates measured on the index stay:

  • CM vs non-CM;
  • AKI, diabetes and cirrhosis, from any diagnosis on the stay;
  • ICU admission (any ICU or CCU days);
  • procedures: lumbar puncture, other spinal, neurosurgical and lung procedures, and ventriculoperitoneal shunt. The shunt list is missing from both the supplement and the reconstruction (a copy/paste loss, in the maintainer’s judgment), so it is rebuilt from the official code sets: ICD-9-CM 02.34 and the nine ICD-10-PCS codes for bypass of a cerebral ventricle to the peritoneal cavity. Open: whether CPT codes, which the paper’s text names, are used as well as ICD procedure codes (#5).

Covariates measured at or before time zero:

  • age; sex and race (USRDS transplant file, record of the most recent transplant; race as White, Black or other); Hispanic ethnicity (patients file, format $YNUFMT: 1 or Y yes, 2 or N no, otherwise missing; the reconstruction treated code 2 as missing); Census region and division of residence (patients file STATE, FIPS codes; as in the reconstruction, Alaska and Hawaii are grouped with the territories as “Outside” rather than in the West, and no one is excluded by geography (decided in #12; the paper’s four regions sum to 1,257 of 1,265, consistent with an unshown Outside/unknown group). Table 1 shows Outside and Unknown as their own rows, subject to small-cell suppression (§10), so the region rows sum to the cohort);
  • BMI, pretransplant dialysis, HIV and CMV serostatus, from the UNOS registration of the most recent transplant on or before the index admission (decided 2026-11-10; the reconstruction used the first transplant’s registration);
  • number of transplants on or before, and graft failures before, the index admission;
  • time since the most recent transplant (also dichotomised at 2 years, as in the paper’s model);
  • acute rejection: any episode (UNOS format ACREJEPI 1 or 2; 3 is none; blank, 990 and 998 missing) at the most recent transplant’s registration or on its follow-up reports dated before the index admission (decided 2026-11-10). A follow-up report without a date takes the kidney graft failure date, as in the reconstruction;
  • any prior hospitalization with a transplant-complication code.

Immunosuppression:

  • antithymocyte globulin, alemtuzumab, anti-IL-2 receptor antibody, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, mycophenolate, mTOR inhibitors, azathioprine, steroids;
  • measured from the UNOS records in the USRDS transplant files, not Medicare drug claims (decided in #2):
    • at transplant: TXIRUNOS, linked to the recipient registration (TXUNOS_TRR_KI/_KP);
    • at first follow-up: TXIFUNOS, from the earliest follow-up report (TXFUUNOS_KI/_KP, earliest FUDATE), whether or not it precedes the index admission, as the paper’s Methods describe (“the first post-transplant visit”). Under OPTN rules this is normally the 6-month report. A drug counts at follow-up if it is current maintenance at that visit (RXMNTCUR);
  • the transplant and its first follow-up are those of the most recent transplant on or before the index admission (decided 2026-11-10); a registration with no drug records is missing, not “not on”;
  • drug classes come from RX_CD and DRUG_CD_OSTXT, using the reconstruction’s mapping, checked against Appendix C, with codes 57 (generic Myfortic), 58 (generic sirolimus) and 59 (Envarsus XR), which it missed, added (decided 2026-11-10).

The paper’s Discussion refers to a “1-year follow-up”, and its Methods and Limitations say the drugs came from “billing codes”; the plan follows the Methods’ time point and the UNOS records. The maintainer notes that billing data may be the gold standard, so this choice may be revisited (#2).

6. Comparator or matching

Not applicable. Comparisons are between people who died within 6 months and those who survived.

7. Outcomes

  • Primary: death from any cause within 180 days of the index admission date (DIED from the patients file), with the admission day as day 0. A death on day 180 counts, and so does a death on the admission day.
  • Secondary:
    • time from index admission to death, censored at the release’s death-date currency (death_data_end_date in R/setup.R, set by whoever runs the analysis for the release in use; the paper does not state it). Every covariate is fixed and death is the only event, so the survival data has one row per person;
    • time from most recent kidney transplant to index admission.

8. Statistical analysis

  • Descriptive:
    • Table 1: characteristics by 6-month vital status;
    • Table 2: immunosuppression by 6-month vital status;
    • continuous variables as mean (SD), and median (IQR) for time since transplant;
    • categorical variables as n (%), with small cells suppressed (§10);
    • missingness reported per variable;
    • tables and figures in the style shared by Open Forum Infectious Diseases, Clinical Infectious Diseases and the Journal of Infectious Diseases, which follow the AMA Manual of Style, 11th edition (OFID author guidelines): P values without a leading zero, confidence intervals written “a to b”, table footnotes marked with lowercase letters, and abbreviations defined in the footnotes.
  • Tests: Student t test for continuous variables; χ² test, or Fisher exact test for sparse tables. Two-sided α = 0.05, with no adjustment for multiple comparisons1.
  • Survival: Kaplan–Meier curve of time to death from the index admission, with a 6-month reference line (Figure 2).
  • Model: logistic regression for 6-month mortality.
    • Every characteristic in Tables 1 and 2 is a candidate (model_candidates in R/setup.R), in the form the paper’s model reports: age as 60 or older1, time since transplant as more than 2 years, and tacrolimus as “not on” at transplant and at follow-up.
    • Each candidate is first tested alone, then all are entered into a multivariable model with backward elimination: the term with the largest P value is removed while P ≥ .05. The paper reports no entry threshold and no forced variables.
    • P values for elimination are joint Wald tests, one per term, as in Stata’s stepwise (the paper’s analysis software). The sample is fixed at the complete cases for all candidates, as Stata does.
    • Fit is assessed by the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, with deciles of predicted risk.
    • Adjusted odds ratios with 95% CIs are shown as a forest plot (Figure 3).
    • The paper’s final model is also fitted as published: its eight terms (cirrhosis, ICU admission, dialysis before transplant, not on tacrolimus at follow-up, age 60 or older, acute kidney injury, more than 2 years since transplant, and an earlier hospitalization for a transplant complication; published_model in R/setup.R)1. It uses the same people as the backward elimination, because the paper’s estimates come from the end of that selection. Table 4 sets its odds ratios beside the published ones, with whether the elimination kept each term. This separates differences in the estimates from differences in which variables the selection keeps.
    • Backward elimination is kept to mirror the paper. Its limitations are noted in §11.
  • Missing data: complete-case, as in the paper. The number excluded from the model is reported.
  • Software: R, with usRds (version and commit recorded with each result), survival, and flowchart for Figure 1.

9. Sensitivity analyses

Variation: explicit Medicare-primary restriction. One way the study could have been done differently is to require Medicare as primary payer from the payer history file (for example on the index admission date, for 365 days before it, or from first transplant to admission), with usRds::verify_medicare_primary(). This tests whether recipients with another primary payer, but a Medicare-paid index stay, change the estimates; it adds a flowchart step the paper does not show (#3).

Alternative: acute rejection from claims. Rejection is measured from UNOS records (§5). The paper’s Methods also mention ICD codes for graft rejection; a claims definition (a hospitalization with ICD-10-CM T86.11, kidney transplant rejection) is an alternative, noted but not built. ICD-9-CM has no rejection-specific kidney code (996.81 covers any complication of a transplanted kidney), so it would apply only from October 2015.

No other sensitivity analyses are prespecified. A comparison of the hospitalization files with institutional claims was considered and not adopted, because the former are a subset of the latter (§3, #1).

10. Disclosure control

The USRDS data use agreement governs. No cell, count or percentage that discloses 1–10 people is published, including counts recoverable by subtraction. Figures, tables and the flowchart stay out of the repository until USRDS review before publication clears them.

11. Limitations to address in reporting

  • Case and CM definitions use diagnosis codes, without microbiological confirmation.
  • Immunosuppression during the index stay is not captured.
  • Stepwise selection gives optimistic P values and unstable selection; the results are exploratory.
  • Restriction to Medicare-paid hospitalizations limits generalisability, and Medicare-primary status is assumed rather than verified (§4).
  • Earlier cryptococcosis (§4, step 4) can only be seen while a person had Medicare before transplant. Recipients on dialysis usually did; those transplanted before dialysis, or with other insurance, may have no earlier claims to find, so the exclusion catches more people who had been on dialysis.
  • Differences from the published estimates may come from the data release, the order of exclusions, the open decisions above, or details of the original Stata code that the paper does not report. They are reported as such, not as corrections to the paper.
